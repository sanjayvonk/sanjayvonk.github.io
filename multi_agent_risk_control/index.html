Distribution-Free Risk Control for Multi-Agent Decision Systems

Sanjay Vonk
Erasmus University Rotterdam · Department of Econometrics
Paper (PDF) Demo (full page)

Abstract

Autonomous systems often couple learned predictions with planning, while safety is judged on realized outcomes under shift and rare failures. We study distribution-free risk control for online multi-agent decision-making with multiple safety objectives. At each time step the system selects actions from a time-varying family of decision rules indexed by a scalar conservatism parameter. Our target is the worst-channel retrospective metric: the time-average of the per-time maximum loss across all agents and objectives. We prove that independent per-channel calibration does not control this metric. We then propose a selective system-level controller that updates a single parameter using the realized worst-channel loss, but applies it only to channels that were recently maximal, leaving others unaffected. Under a mild delayed-safety condition, we obtain a finite-time worst-channel bound without distributional assumptions. We instantiate the theory in a drone–pedestrian navigation simulation with inertia, short-horizon prediction, and spline planning.

Problem setup

We consider an online decision system over discrete times \(t = 1,2,\dots,T\). At time \(t\) the system observes an input \(x_t\in\mathcal{X}\), chooses an action \(u_t\in\mathcal{U}\), and then an outcome \(y_t\in\mathcal{Y}\) is realized. The only standing requirement is bounded loss: \[ \mathcal{L}(u_t, y_t) \in [0,1], \] and we make no stochastic assumptions on the sequence \((x_t,y_t)\): it may be nonstationary, non-exchangeable, or even adversarial.

The system does not pick arbitrary actions. Instead, at each time \(t\) it has a family of decision rules indexed by a single scalar parameter: \[ \mathcal{D}_t := \{ D_t^{\lambda} : \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{U} \mid \lambda \in \mathbb{R} \}. \] You can think of \(\lambda\) as a continuously-tunable knob that moves the planner from aggressive (high throughput) to cautious (large safety margins).

Multi-agent systems bring multiple channels of safety loss. Index channels by \(c\in\mathcal{C}\) (for example, “drone 7 vs pedestrians” or “drone 3 vs drone 4”). Let \(\ell_t^c\in[0,1]\) be the loss on channel \(c\) at time \(t\). The worst-channel loss and the metric we control are: \[ m_t := \max_{c\in\mathcal{C}} \ell_t^c, \qquad \widehat{R}^{\max}_T := \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T m_t. \] This captures the realized frequency/severity of the most critical safety events across the entire system.

Selective worst-channel control

A naive global strategy is to share one parameter across all channels and update it using the worst loss \(m_t\). This yields a clean guarantee, but it can induce an undesirable coupling: a single near-miss in one corner can slow everyone down.

Our controller preserves the system-level guarantee while localizing the behavioral effect. We maintain a global parameter \(\lambda^G_t\) and per-channel local parameters \(\{\lambda^c_t\}_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\). At each time \(t\), we choose an active set \(\mathcal{A}_t\subseteq\mathcal{C}\) using only past information (e.g., recent maximizers, a predictive critical set, top-\(k\) channels). The planner uses the effective parameter \[ \lambda^{\mathrm{eff},c}_t := \begin{cases} \lambda^G_t, & c \in \mathcal{A}_t,\\ \lambda^c_t, & c \notin \mathcal{A}_t. \end{cases} \] Then we update the global parameter by one-dimensional feedback on the realized worst-channel loss: \[ \lambda^G_{t+1} = \lambda^G_t + \eta\bigl(\varepsilon - m_t\bigr), \] where \(\varepsilon\in[0,1]\) is the target risk level and \(\eta>0\) is a step size.

Interpretation. The update controls a system-level quantity (so it is not diluted by the number of agents), while the active set makes the planner feel this conservatism only where it matters. In the demo, this is why the global controller can look “overly cautious,” while the selective controller stays fast except at the specific moments/channels that repeatedly dominate worst-case risk.

Distribution-free guarantee

The analysis is planner-agnostic: the decision rules \(D_t^{\lambda}\) may be nonconvex, heuristic, and may incorporate prediction, coupling between agents, and an arbitrary measurable active-set policy. The only substantive modeling requirement is a mild delayed safety condition (natural for inertial systems), which says that sufficiently conservative parameters yield low average worst-channel loss after at most \(K\) steps.

Under delayed safety and bounded losses \(m_t\in[0,1]\), the global feedback update yields a finite-time bound: \[ \widehat{R}^{\max}_t \le \varepsilon + \frac{\lambda^G_1 - \lambda_{\mathrm{safe}}}{\eta\,t} + \frac{K}{t}, \qquad t\ge K. \] In particular, \(\limsup_{t\to\infty} \widehat{R}^{\max}_t \le \varepsilon\). This is fully distribution-free: no IID, no exchangeability, no probabilistic model of errors.

Application: multi-drone navigation among pedestrians

The demo instantiates the abstract objects above in a setting designed to be visually interpretable: drones have inertia, make short-horizon plans under imperfect constant-velocity prediction, and face two simultaneous safety objectives.

Inertial dynamics. Each drone has position \(p_t\in\mathbb{R}^2\) and velocity \(v_t\in\mathbb{R}^2\). The planner chooses a desired velocity \(v_t^{\mathrm{des}}\), tracked with a first-order response: \[ a_t = \mathrm{clip}\left(\frac{v_t^{\mathrm{des}}-v_t}{\tau},\,a_{\max}\right),\quad v_{t+1} = \mathrm{clip}(v_t + \Delta t\,a_t,\,v_{\max}),\quad p_{t+1}=p_t+\Delta t\,v_{t+1}. \]

Channels and bounded losses. For drone \(i\), let \(d_{t,\min}^{(i,\mathrm{ped})}\) be its distance to the nearest pedestrian and \(d_{t,\min}^{(i,\mathrm{dd})}\) the distance to the nearest other drone. Using separation targets \(d_{\mathrm{ped}}\) and \(d_{\mathrm{dd}}\), we use normalized margin violations: \[ \ell_t^{(i,\mathrm{ped})} = \min\Bigl\{1,\,\max\bigl\{0,\tfrac{d_{\mathrm{ped}} - d_{t,\min}^{(i,\mathrm{ped})}}{d_{\mathrm{ped}}}\bigr\}\Bigr\},\quad \ell_t^{(i,\mathrm{dd})} = \min\Bigl\{1,\,\max\bigl\{0,\tfrac{d_{\mathrm{dd}} - d_{t,\min}^{(i,\mathrm{dd})}}{d_{\mathrm{dd}}}\bigr\}\Bigr\}. \]

What to look for in the demo

  • No risk control (blue): stays aggressive and often collides, losing progress and reward.
  • Global shared parameter (purple): suppresses collisions by slowing everyone down, even when only one channel is risky.
  • Selective worst-channel control (orange): matches the blue controller most of the time, then becomes conservative only when a specific channel becomes critical—preserving throughput.

The guarantee concerns the worst-channel metric \(\widehat{R}^{\max}_T\), and the key empirical question is whether we can reduce the largest safety violations without imposing a uniform slowdown. The selective controller is designed for exactly this tradeoff.
